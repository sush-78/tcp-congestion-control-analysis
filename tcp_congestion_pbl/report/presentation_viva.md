# Viva Questions and Answers
**1. What is TCP congestion control?** It regulates how much data a sender injects into the network to respond to congestion.

**2. What is cwnd?** The sender's congestion window, expressed here in MSS units.

**3. What is ssthresh?** The threshold between slow start and congestion avoidance in the simplified model.

**4. What is slow start?** A phase of rapid, approximately exponential congestion-window growth per RTT.

**5. What is congestion avoidance?** A phase of more gradual, approximately additive window growth.

**6. How do Tahoe and Reno differ?** Classic Tahoe returns cwnd to one MSS after loss; classic Reno can use fast recovery after triple duplicate ACKs, while timeout recovery returns to slow start.

**7. What is RTT?** Round-trip time for data/feedback exchange; this project uses a configured RTT.

**8. Is packet loss real here?** No, losses are generated by a seeded random model.

**9. How is throughput calculated?** Delivered payload bits divided by simulation duration, converted to Mbps.

**10. Why use a seed?** To make pseudo-random outcomes reproducible.

**11. Does Reno always perform better?** No; outcomes depend on model assumptions and network parameters.

**12. What are the limitations?** No live packet transmission, and ACK/retransmission behavior is simplified.

**13. Why Streamlit?** It provides Python-based controls, charts, tables, and downloads.

**14. Why separate modules?** Separation makes simulation, metrics, and UI easier to test and maintain.

**15. What is future scope?** Detailed ACK modeling, multiple-seed statistics, and controlled packet-trace comparison.
